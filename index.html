Integration notes

  • Integration is total stacked time at 10–20 s subs. Start at the low end; go to the high end (or past it) for faint arms, tails, halos and outer nebulosity.
  • Filter tags: UV/IR = the standard UV/IR-cut filter (galaxies, reflection nebulae, clusters). Dual-band = the built-in Ha/OIII filter (emission nebulae, planetaries, supernova remnants), which also cuts moonlight and light pollution.
  • Framing tags compare each target with the selected scope's field: Fits (any rotation), Fits lengthwise (fits when the field's long side lines up, which changes through the night in alt-az mode), Small (fits easily; crop afterward), or the mosaic size needed. Seestar's mosaic mode can capture those.
  • Integration times are set for the S30 Pro.
  • Split long totals across several nights in the same dark window and stack them. Battery is about 6 h, so plan one or two targets per night.
  • Targets tagged “near zenith” climb past 80°, where alt-az tracking struggles. Image them a couple of hours either side of transit, or use EQ mode on a wedge.
  • Max alt shows how high a target climbs during full darkness tonight (this is what the Max altitude sort uses), with its best for the next 12 months underneath as “year”. Neither counts daylight or twilight.
  • Amber “low” targets never clear 30° in darkness from this location. Image them within an hour or so of transit with a clean horizon in their direction.
  • Greyed-out targets never rise high enough to image from this location.
  • Red light: the ● Red light button turns the page into red-only light to protect your night vision at the telescope. Your phone's own menus and the browser bar aren't affected; for full coverage also use the phone's red color filter (iPhone: Settings → Accessibility → Display & Text Size → Color Filters → Color Tint).
  • Moon distance: a bright Moon is acceptable when it stays well away from the target: at least 60° with the dual-band filter, which blocks most moonlight, and at least 90° with the UV/IR-cut filter. Dark windows count those nights, and each chart says how close the Moon comes tonight. The Moon distance sort lists the safest targets tonight first.
  • The Min altitude setting (default 25°) is the lowest altitude counted as imaging time. It changes the seasons, peak nights, dark windows, the chart's threshold line and the hours in darkness. Targets that never climb at least 10° above it use a threshold 8° below their highest point instead.
  • Each chart shows the target's altitude tonight (red), the Moon (grey dashed) and the sky from daylight to full dark. Hover or drag across a chart to read the altitude, direction and time at any moment.

Season = months averaging at least half of the target's best-night dark hours. Peak = the night the target is up longest in full darkness. Dates are local calendar nights, labeled by the evening date. Sun and Moon use low-precision formulas, good to a few minutes. Positions J2000; sizes and magnitudes are approximate.
